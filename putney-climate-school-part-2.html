My Two Weeks at the Putney Columbia Climate School (Part 2)

August 2, 2026

Picking up where I left off in Part 1, the second week of the Putney Columbia Climate School was just as exciting as the first. We spent more time working on our Climate Action Projects (CAPs), attended more lectures, and continued learning through hands-on activities. Every day brought something new, and I was excited to see what the rest of the program had in store.

Day 5: Discovering How Nature Holds Climate Clues

Day 5 started with more work on our Climate Action Project (CAP), where we focused on coming up with solutions. After that, we attended a lecture on climate change communication with Josh DeVincenzo, where we learned how important it is to make climate science understandable and engaging for different audiences.

Next, we had a lecture on proxy data analysis with Laurel Zaima-Sheehy and Emma Kyziva. We learned about different types of trees and how scientists use tree coring to estimate their age. One thing that surprised me was that a smaller tree core doesn't necessarily mean the tree is younger—it could just mean the tree grew more slowly because it was weaker or faced tougher conditions. Then, we got to try tree coring ourselves, which was a really fun hands-on experience.

After that, we worked on our CAP again before heading to our afternoon activity, Roommate Olympics! Since I didn't have a roommate, I was paired with someone from another floor who also didn't have one. It was a little awkward at first, but I ended up enjoying it because it gave me the chance to meet someone new.

Day 6: Seeing Climate Action in Local Communities

Day 6 was our trip to Montpelier, Vermont's capital! We visited the State House, where we learned about the history of the building, the purpose of each room, and why they are important to Vermont's government.

After that, we had the opportunity to meet with Representative Emily Carris-Duncan. It was so nice getting to talk with them, especially after learning that they became involved in politics because they saw how climate change was affecting their hometown in Vermont. Hearing about their experiences made climate policy feel much more personal and inspiring.

After our meeting, we had lunch at a nearby pho restaurant before heading back to Castleton. That evening, we had our own version of The Great British Bake Off! Each biome group had to decorate a treat based on their assigned biome. I was in the Tundra biome, so I made an Arctic fox. I didn't win, but the person who did definitely deserved it!

Day 7: Understanding Global Climate Decisions

Day 7 was filled with lectures about climate policy and international cooperation. We learned about the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), the Paris Agreement, decarbonization, and global climate governance from experts Martin Dietrich Brauch and Ben Baraga.

Later, we took part in a climate change policy and negotiations simulation led by Martin, Ben, Laurel, and our facilitators.

During the simulation, we pretended we were delegates at the United Nations negotiating climate policy. I was part of the Developed Nations group, and we worked with other groups representing developing nations, China, the United States, and the European Union to try to reach the goal of limiting global warming to 1.5°C.

It was really interesting to see how difficult it can be to balance different countries' priorities while still working toward a common goal.

After the simulation, we spent more time working on our Climate Action Project before heading to our evening activities. I went out to the field and played volleyball with everyone, which was a fun way to end a busy day.

Day 8: Exploring Solutions to a Changing Climate

Day 8 focused on different solutions to climate change and how countries, governments, and businesses can help reduce emissions. Throughout the day, we attended several lectures with Martin Dietrich Brauch about decarbonization, climate policy, and the role of the private sector in climate action.

In the afternoon, my biome group participated in a renewable energy demonstration, where we learned how different renewable energy sources work together to power an electrical grid. We also took part in the Decarbonization Challenge, which brought together many of the ideas we had learned throughout the day.

After dinner, we met with our biome groups before heading to our evening activities. I chose to go to the Secret Santa craft night, where we made postcards for each other as part of a Secret Santa activity. It was a relaxing way to end the day and a fun opportunity to spend more time with everyone.

Day 9: Rethinking the Way We Use Resources

Day 9 was all about the circular economy, which is a system that focuses on reducing waste by reusing, repairing, and recycling materials instead of simply throwing them away.

We started with an introduction to the concept from Sandra Goldmark before participating in a "From Linear to Circular" activity. Then, we split into groups and came up with our own business ideas based on circular economy principles. My group focused on retrofitting apartment buildings to make them more sustainable and energy-efficient.

After everyone finished, the top four groups were selected to present their ideas. The winning project was a business that created customizable jackets, which was a really creative way to reduce clothing waste.

Later, we spent more time working on our Climate Action Project before ending the day by watching Sheep Detectives, which was a fun way to relax after another busy day.

Continuing the Journey

By the end of Day 9, it was hard to believe how much we had already learned in just over a week. Every lecture, activity, and conversation gave me a new perspective on climate change and the many ways people around the world are working to address it.

At the same time, I was becoming even closer with everyone in the program, making each day even more enjoyable.

With a few days still left at the Putney Columbia Climate School, there was still so much to look forward to. From finishing our Climate Action Projects to more field trips and activities, the experience was far from over.

Thank you for reading, and stay tuned for Part 3!
